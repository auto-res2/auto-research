
# ProtoSurvPath: A Prototype-Based Multimodal Framework for Oncological Survival Prediction

## Abstract
Integrating complex modalities such as whole‐slide images (WSIs) and bulk transcriptomics for survival prediction poses significant computational and interpretative challenges. In this work, we introduce ProtoSurvPath, a unified, prototype‐based multimodal framework that simultaneously learns compact, data‐driven representations for both transcriptomics and histology images to improve prognostic accuracy in oncology. Our approach leverages a dedicated gene expression encoder together with a pretrained vision encoder to address the high dimensionality and disparate scales inherent to these modalities. Specifically, bulk transcriptomic data are first transformed through a neural encoder and then processed by an unsupervised Gaussian mixture model (GMM) to extract latent transcriptomic prototypes that capture disease‐specific gene expression patterns beyond conventional fixed pathway tokenizations. In parallel, whole‐slide image patches are embedded using a modern vision backbone and subsequently summarized into morphological prototypes by applying an analogous clustering strategy. A lightweight cross‐attention Transformer fuses these two sets of prototypes by explicitly learning dense interactions between them, producing a final survival risk score while simultaneously yielding attention maps that offer biologically interpretable associations between genotype and phenotype. Extensive experiments on multiple The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) datasets—including BLCA, BRCA, STAD, COADREAD, and HNSC—employing 5‐fold cross‐validation and survival metrics such as the concordance index (c‐index) and time‐dependent area under the curve (AUC) demonstrate that ProtoSurvPath consistently outperforms baseline methods, such as the previously proposed SURVPATH, a Base Method based on fixed pathway tokens, and a variant inspired by PANTHER. Paired t-tests confirm that these improvements are statistically significant, particularly in scenarios with limited training data where overfitting is a serious concern. An ablation study further reveals that removing either the transcriptomic or morphological prototyping, or replacing the cross‐attention fusion module with simple feature concatenation, markedly degrades performance and diminishes the model’s interpretability. Our methodological contributions can be summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Prototype-Based Tokenization:} We propose a data-driven approach that compresses high-dimensional transcriptomic and histological information into compact, informative prototypes via neural encoding followed by unsupervised Gaussian mixture clustering. This strategy not only reduces noise and alleviates the computational burden but also facilitates the discovery of latent, disease-specific biomarkers that exceed the representational capacity of traditional fixed pathway tokenizations.
\item \textbf{Cross-Modal Attention Fusion:} We introduce a lightweight Transformer-based fusion module that explicitly models dense interactions between transcriptomic and morphological prototypes. This cross-attention mechanism enhances survival prediction accuracy while generating interpretable attention maps that elucidate the mechanistic relationships between gene expression patterns and tissue morphology, thereby providing clinically actionable insights.
\item \textbf{Robust Survival Prediction:} By integrating dual prototyping with an end-to-end trainable survival risk prediction head, ProtoSurvPath achieves state-of-the-art prognostic performance relative to unimodal and conventional multimodal baselines. This robustness is particularly evident in settings with moderately sized datasets where overfitting has traditionally limited predictive accuracy.
\item \textbf{Enhanced Interpretability:} Our framework incorporates techniques such as integrated gradients and prototype assignment mapping to transparently identify the transcriptomic features that interact with specific morphological characteristics. These interpretable visualizations not only facilitate biomarker discovery but also support clinicians in making more informed decisions regarding targeted therapeutic interventions.
\end{itemize}
The entire experimental pipeline has been implemented using standard Python libraries, including PyTorch for deep learning, scikit-learn for clustering and statistical analyses, and Matplotlib/Seaborn for visualization. The training loop optimizes a survival loss function – which in clinical applications can be replaced by a negative Cox partial log-likelihood – and employs early stopping based on validation performance to mitigate overfitting on moderately sized datasets. Our framework eliminates the reliance on predefined biological pathways by compressing heterogeneous data into low-noise prototypes and leveraging a cross-modal attention mechanism to delineate the interaction between gene expression and tumor morphology. This integrated approach not only leads to superior prognostic accuracy but also paves the way for future biomarker discovery and the development of targeted therapeutics. Moreover, our comprehensive experimental evaluation—including rigorous ablation studies—demonstrates that each component of ProtoSurvPath is critical to its overall performance and interpretability. In particular, the dual-prototyping strategy captures the underlying biological heterogeneity inherent in complex clinical data, while the cross-attention fusion module provides a novel means to unravel the mechanistic interplay between molecular and morphological features. These findings suggest that ProtoSurvPath offers a robust, interpretable, and clinically relevant solution for survival prediction in oncology. Overall, our results underscore the potential of prototype-based multimodal integration to transform prognostic modeling and drive forward the discovery of actionable biomarkers, ultimately leading to improved patient stratification and personalized treatment strategies in the field of cancer research.
